## Craft: adding soul

Removing patterns is half the job; sterile voiceless writing is equally obvious. Where personality fits (blogs, essays, opinions; NOT reference, legal, technical docs):

- Have opinions. React, don't report. "This API design is frustrating" beats "the API has certain limitations".
- Calibrate certainty across the real spectrum: "no question" when sure, "I think" sometimes, "I'm not sure, but" when doubting. AI parks in flat medium confidence; never stack hedges.
- Be specific: numbers, names, mechanisms. "Cut p99 latency from 900ms to 40ms" beats "significantly improves performance". The portability test: if a sentence could move unchanged to another company or product, it is filler.
- Include friction: "The RPC kept timing out at 3am and I nearly scrapped the whole feature."
- Use contractions. Reference real time and place. Occasionally start sentences with And or But.
- Let sentences be ugly sometimes: fragments, run-ons that keep going because the thought isn't done.
- Choose the second or third word that comes to mind, not the most statistically likely one.
- End without wrapping up. Sometimes just stop.

Voice profiles on request: casual (contractions, first person, fragments), professional (selective contractions, dry wit), technical (precise terms, numbers over adjectives, no metaphor unless clarifying), warm (we language, acknowledges difficulty), blunt (shortest sentences, no hedging). Match register to context; do not neutralize toward a generic middle.

Cut stock similes. "The air hung over the table like a warm cloth" names no observed detail and does not match any author's voice. Keep a comparison only when it shows something the plain statement cannot.
